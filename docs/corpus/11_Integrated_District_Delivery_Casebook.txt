Integrated District Delivery Casebook
====================================

Scope and status
----------------
The Integrated District Delivery Pilot combines selected rural roads, rooftop
solar support, and digital benefit payments in the Sundar and Narmada districts.
It is a planning and monitoring casebook, not a national programme report. The
figures below are provisional administrative figures for financial year 2024-25
unless a different period is stated.

Funding and accounting
----------------------
The approved pilot envelope was Rs 48 crore. Rs 31 crore had been released by
31 March 2025 and reported expenditure was Rs 27.4 crore. Approval, release and
expenditure are different measures and should not be treated as interchangeable.
The expenditure figure includes district coordination and payment-system costs,
not only household transfers.

Household registration and verification
---------------------------------------
The district offices registered 18,400 households for at least one intervention.
After duplicate removal and document checks, 15,920 households were verified.
The monitoring dashboard recorded 12,600 households as having received at least
one benefit. These counts describe different stages of the process: registered,
verified and benefit-receiving. They are not three estimates of the same outcome.

Rooftop solar component
-----------------------
There were 4,800 solar applications across the two districts. Distribution
companies had commissioned systems for 3,100 households by the reporting date.
The remaining applications were not necessarily rejected: 1,050 were awaiting
technical inspection, 420 were waiting for an applicant contribution, and 230
were held for documentation or address correction. A commissioned system is
counted only after installation inspection; an approved application is counted
earlier.

Rural access works
------------------
The pilot schedule listed 86 kilometres of rural access improvements. Engineers
certified 62 kilometres as complete, while 14 kilometres were under construction
and 10 kilometres had not started. The completed length is an engineering
progress measure and does not by itself establish that every target household
has improved market or health-service access.

Digital payments and exceptions
-------------------------------
Benefit payments were routed through bank accounts and assisted service points.
Of the 12,600 households recorded as receiving a benefit, 1,180 experienced at
least one failed or delayed transaction. The casebook does not say that all of
these households permanently lost a benefit. Common exceptions were account
mapping errors, inactive accounts, name mismatches and intermittent connectivity.
District teams used a correction queue and a grievance desk, but the casebook
does not provide a final resolution rate.

Time and target caveats
-----------------------
The pilot had a planning target of 25,000 registered households for 2025-26.
That is a target, not a reported achievement. No final 2025-26 coverage,
district ranking, ministerial assignment or audit finding is provided here.
The two districts used the same dashboard, but their local implementation
capacity and contractor schedules were not identical.

Interpretation note
-------------------
When a question asks for "coverage," clarify whether it means registered,
verified or benefit-receiving households. When it asks for "budget," clarify
whether it means approved, released or spent funds. When it asks which district
performed better, identify the metric first; the casebook does not provide a
single composite performance score.
